Savings rate vs return rate: Zaid vs Amina

How this was researched and written

The honest version of this comparison does not have a tidy moral, and the untidy part is the useful part.

A beginner's first instinct is to spend their energy on the return. Which fund, which stock, which strategy. It feels like the lever that matters, because it is the one everybody talks about.

Start with the smallest possible version of the question. You have $10,000 invested for a year.

ReturnAfter one yearDifference
9%$10,900
10%$11,000$100

A full extra percentage point of return, which is a serious and genuinely difficult achievement, is worth $100. Cancelling one subscription and one takeaway a month is also worth $100, and takes an afternoon. The second lever is not smaller than the first. At this stage it is larger, and it is entirely within your control, which the return is not.

That is the standard lesson and it is true. Now the interesting part, because the standard lesson stops too early.

Two approaches, and only one of them is hard work

Both Zaid and Amina are 30, both earn $80,000 gross and $63,880 net, and both are investing. They are going about it in opposite ways.

  • Zaid takes the effortful path. He researches individual companies, picks the ones he believes will outperform, and follows them. He invests $10,000 a year. This is genuinely a lot of work, and it is the version of investing that feels serious.
  • Amina takes the easy path. She cuts back in a few places that do not really register, a couple of meals out a month and a subscription she was not watching, and puts $12,000 a year into a single low-cost, Shariah-screened ETF (a basket of many companies in one purchase) at an ordinary 10%. She then does nothing at all.
Now give Zaid a return nobody actually gets. For this one comparison, assume Zaid's stock picking delivers 20% a year. That number is deliberately unrealistic. It is roughly double the long-run market return and it would put him among a tiny handful of investors globally. It is set that high on purpose, because the argument is stronger if the exaggeration runs against it. Everywhere else in this course, Zaid earns the same ordinary 10% as Amina.

Before you look: who is ahead after five years?

Zaid is earning double the return on a slightly smaller contribution. Amina is earning an ordinary return on $2,000 a year more. Commit to an answer before you scroll: who is ahead after five years, and by how much? Most people get the direction right and the size badly wrong, in both directions.

AfterZaid: $10,000/yr at an unrealistic 20%Amina: $12,000/yr at an ordinary 10%Ahead
1 year$12,000$13,200Amina, by $1,200
2 years$26,400$27,720Amina, by $1,320
3 years$43,680$43,692Amina, by $12
4 years$64,416$61,261Zaid, by $3,155
5 years$89,299$80,587Zaid, by $8,712

Read that carefully, because it says something more useful than a tidy moral would. Amina, doing nothing clever at all, stays ahead of a man earning double her return for three full years. It takes until year four for an impossible return to overtake an extra $2,000 a year of saving, and even at five years the gap is $8,712, on an assumption that is not real.

What that actually means for where you put your effort

Zaid's 20% is not available. That is the point of setting it so high: even granting him a return that essentially nobody sustains, the first several years belong to the person who simply saved more. And the first several years are where almost every reader of this course currently is.

The realistic version is worse for him again. Strip the fantasy out, give Zaid the same 10% Amina gets, and he loses at every single horizon, permanently, by exactly the margin her higher savings rate bought her. Nothing clever happened. She saved more.

  • Early on, your savings rate is the whole game. There is not yet enough capital for a percentage point of return to matter. An extra $2,000 a year beats a return nobody can actually deliver.
  • The effort is asymmetric, which is the part people miss. Amina's edge came from skipping a few meals out and setting up an automatic transfer. Zaid's required continuous research, and it still lost for three years. One of those is repeatable for thirty years and the other is not.
  • Past roughly $100,000 of capital, the balance shifts. Once the pile is large, a percentage point is worth more than any plausible change to what you can save, and it becomes genuinely worth spending time on how the money is invested rather than only on how much of it there is. Even then, broad index funds tend to win on risk-adjusted return, because even the top professional managers rarely beat the market consistently.
  • So the practical order is: increase your income, increase the gap between earning and spending, then invest it simply. That sequence is boring, it is entirely within your control, and it is what actually produces the outcome.
Name the red flag plainly. A specific, high, forward-looking return number in marketing material is a warning sign in its own right, separate from whether it is achievable. Reputable funds describe strategy, risk and cost, and present past performance with its dates attached and a statement that it does not predict future performance. If a product leads with a number, ask three things: over what period, after what fees, and what happened to the funds that were being marketed the same way ten years ago.

Run your own version with your own contribution and assumption on the growth calculator. Put in what you can actually save at 10%, then try a return you would never really get, and see how many years it takes for the second one to matter.

Check yourself

3 questions on this lesson. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

  1. 1In the five-year comparison, Zaid is given a 20% return. Why that number?

    The point is that even a return essentially nobody sustains does not beat a higher savings rate for the first few years.

  2. 2For how long does Amina stay ahead, despite earning half the return?

    She is ahead through year three. Zaid only overtakes in year four, and only because of a return that is not actually available.

  3. 3At roughly what point does it start being worth spending real effort on investment selection?

    Below that, a percentage point is small in dollar terms relative to what you can change by saving more. Above it, the arithmetic shifts.
